
# Lie-Algebraic Controllability of NV–¹⁴N Spin Systems

## Overview

This directory contains numerical implementations for investigating the controllability of a nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centre coupled to a nitrogen-14 nuclear spin.

Both the electronic and nuclear spins are treated as spin-1 systems. The composite Hilbert space is therefore nine-dimensional:

$$
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_e\otimes\mathcal{H}_N,
$$

$$
\dim(\mathcal{H})=3\times3=9.
$$

The main objective is to construct the dynamical Lie algebra generated by the drift and control Hamiltonians under different magnetic-field conditions and control configurations.

## Physical Model

The spin-1 electronic operators are represented by

$$
S_x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
S_y=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-i&0\\
i&0&-i\\
0&i&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
S_z=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The nuclear-spin operators $I_x$, $I_y$, and $I_z$ have the same spin-1 matrix representation.

The drift Hamiltonian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0={}&
D S_z^2\otimes\mathbb{I}_3
+Q\mathbb{I}_3\otimes I_z^2\\
&+A_\perp(S_x\otimes I_x+S_y\otimes I_y)\\
&+A_\parallel S_z\otimes I_z\\
&+\gamma_e B S_z\otimes\mathbb{I}_3\\
&+\gamma_N B\mathbb{I}_3\otimes I_z.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here:

- $D$ is the electronic zero-field splitting.
- $Q$ is the nuclear quadrupole parameter.
- $A_\perp$ is the transverse hyperfine coupling.
- $A_\parallel$ is the longitudinal hyperfine coupling.
- $\gamma_e$ and $\gamma_N$ are the electronic and nuclear gyromagnetic ratios.
- $B$ is the applied magnetic field.

The Hamiltonian coefficients are expressed in GHz, and the magnetic field is expressed in Tesla.

## Dynamical Lie-Algebra Construction

For a controlled Hamiltonian of the form

$$
H(t)=H_0+\sum_k u_k(t)H_k,
$$

the corresponding anti-Hermitian generators are defined as

$$
A_0=-iH_0,
\qquad
A_k=-iH_k.
$$

The dynamical Lie algebra is generated by these matrices and their nested commutators:

$$
[A,B]=AB-BA.
$$

### Numerical Method

The numerical implementation constructs the algebra iteratively using the following procedure:

1. Construct the drift and control Hamiltonians using NumPy.
2. Generate their tensor-product representations using `numpy.kron`.
3. Convert the Hamiltonian generators into anti-Hermitian matrices.
4. Calculate nested commutators between the current generators and the initial generators.
5. Test linear independence using the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product and a Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization procedure.
6. Repeat the process until no new independent generators are obtained or the maximum dimension of 81 is reached.

The inner product used in the implementation is

$$
\langle A,B\rangle
=
\mathrm{Re}
\left[
\mathrm{Tr}(A^\dagger B)
\right].
$$

The orthogonalization procedure performs two passes to reduce numerical errors.

For a candidate matrix $M$, its projections onto the previously obtained orthonormal basis matrices are subtracted. The norm of the remaining matrix is then evaluated. A candidate is retained when its residual norm exceeds the specified relative tolerance.

The relative tolerance used in the independence test is

$$
\varepsilon=10^{-12}.
$$

The maximum dimension considered is

$$
\dim\mathfrak{u}(9)=9^2=81.
$$

The calculation records the number of independent generators obtained at each commutator depth.

## Control Configurations

Four configurations are investigated.

### 1. Zero Field — Electronic $S_x$ and $S_y$ Controls

The control Hamiltonian is

$$
H_c(t)=
u_x(t)S_x\otimes\mathbb{I}_3
+
u_y(t)S_y\otimes\mathbb{I}_3.
$$

This configuration investigates whether electronic-spin control alone generates the full dynamical Lie algebra of the coupled nine-dimensional system.

The numerical calculation reaches a Lie-algebra dimension of

$$
\dim\mathcal{L}=81.
$$

### 2. Zero Field — Electronic $S_x$ and Nuclear $I_x$ Controls

The control Hamiltonian is

$$
H_c(t)=
u_x(t)S_x\otimes\mathbb{I}_3
+
u'_x(t)\mathbb{I}_3\otimes I_x.
$$

The numerical calculation gives a Lie-algebra dimension of

$$
\dim\mathcal{L}=40.
$$

This restricted dimension is associated with a symmetry of the coupled Hamiltonian. The symmetry decomposes the Hilbert space into invariant subspaces of dimensions 5 and 4:

$$
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_+\oplus\mathcal{H}_-.
$$

The corresponding algebra is

$$
\mathfrak{su}(5)\oplus
\mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus
\mathfrak{u}(1).
$$

Its dimension is

$$
(5^2-1)+(4^2-1)+1=40.
$$

### 3. Finite Magnetic Field — Electronic $S_x$ Control

A longitudinal magnetic field is introduced, with both electronic and nuclear Zeeman contributions included in the drift Hamiltonian.

The control Hamiltonian is

$$
H_c(t)=u_x(t)S_x\otimes\mathbb{I}_3.
$$

The magnetic-field contributions are

$$
H_B=
\gamma_e B S_z\otimes\mathbb{I}_3
+
\gamma_N B\mathbb{I}_3\otimes I_z.
$$

For the field value used in the implementation,

$$
B=10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T},
$$

the numerical calculation reaches a Lie-algebra dimension of

$$
\dim\mathcal{L}=81.
$$

### 4. Finite Magnetic Field — Neglecting the Nuclear Zeeman Contribution

The final configuration retains the electronic Zeeman contribution but sets the nuclear gyromagnetic ratio to zero:

$$
\gamma_N=0.
$$

The control Hamiltonian remains

$$
H_c(t)=u_x(t)S_x\otimes\mathbb{I}_3.
$$

The numerical calculation again reaches a Lie-algebra dimension of

$$
\dim\mathcal{L}=81.
$$

This configuration allows comparison with the preceding case to examine the role of the nuclear Zeeman contribution in the generated Lie algebra.

## Numerical Results

The calculated dimensions for the four configurations are summarized below.

| Configuration | Lie-algebra dimension |
|---|---:|
| Zero field, electronic $S_x,S_y$ controls | 81 |
| Zero field, electronic $S_x$ and nuclear $I_x$ controls | 40 |
| Finite field, electronic $S_x$ control, both Zeeman terms | 81 |
| Finite field, electronic $S_x$ control, no nuclear Zeeman term | 81 |

A dimension of 81 corresponds to the full $\mathfrak{u}(9)$ algebra in the numerical analysis, whereas the 40-dimensional result reflects the symmetry-restricted algebra in the second configuration.

## Scripts

### 1. Zero-Field Electronic $S_x,S_y$ Controls

`zero_field_electron_xy.py`

Constructs the dynamical Lie algebra for the coupled system at zero magnetic field with two electronic-spin control Hamiltonians.

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python scripts/nv14_controllability/zero_field_electron_xy.py
```

### 2. Zero-Field Electronic $S_x$ and Nuclear $I_x$ Controls

`zero_field_electron_x_nuclear_x.py`

Investigates the coupled system with independent electronic and nuclear control Hamiltonians.

Run:

```bash
python scripts/nv14_controllability/zero_field_electron_x_nuclear_x.py
```

### 3. Finite Field with Both Zeeman Contributions

`field_electron_x.py`

Calculates the dynamical Lie algebra with a finite longitudinal magnetic field and an electronic $S_x$ control.

Run:

```bash
python scripts/nv14_controllability/field_electron_x.py
```

### 4. Finite Field Without Nuclear Zeeman Contribution

`field_electron_x_no_nuclear_zeeman.py`

Repeats the finite-field calculation after neglecting the nuclear Zeeman contribution.

Run:

```bash
python scripts/nv14_controllability/field_electron_x_no_nuclear_zeeman.py
```

## Computational Notes

The Lie-algebra dimensions are determined numerically using finite-precision matrix operations. The independence test therefore depends on the selected numerical tolerance.

The code records the number of independent generators at each commutator depth and prints the final dimension of the generated algebra.

The numerical results characterize the specified Hamiltonian models and control configurations.

## Future Development

Further investigations may examine the sensitivity of the generated Lie algebras to changes in Hamiltonian parameters, magnetic-field orientation, and additional control configurations.

The connection between controllability and open-system quantum dynamics is also a potential direction for extending this work.
